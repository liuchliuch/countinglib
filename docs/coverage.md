# Scope and mathematical relationships

CountingLib organizes reusable infrastructure around complete classification
and representation results. A classification specialization, a computational
reduction, a proof dependency, and shared infrastructure express different
relationships.

| Relationship | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Specialization | A theorem instantiated at the stated language, domain, resources, and graph class |
| Reduction | A concrete instance map or oracle machine, value/count recovery, representations, promises, and costs |
| Proof dependency | An actual Lean import or declaration dependency |
| Shared infrastructure | Common definitions or algorithms, without a claim of mathematical implication |

## Established bridges

- Relational indicator partitions equal accepted-assignment and witness counts.
- Natural-weight multigraph homomorphism instances reduce to independent CSP inputs.
- Original Holant, ComplexCSP, and PlanarHom models connect to public partition semantics.
- Product, Affine, and variable-elimination execution connect to S1 partitions.
- Paired-stub open-network raw values, corrected tensors, and composition connect to S1.
- Exact fixed gadgets support resource elimination on their stated original encodings.
- Standard binary #P witnesses connect to accepting paths and independent counting NOR.
- Parsimonious reductions transport standard binary #P membership through actual machines.
- Fixed-palette Product inputs use the original finite solver and codec.
- CPOG counters and exact matchgate compression have public semantic consumers.

## Boundaries

The Boolean Holant results do not classify arbitrary domains. Fixed finite
resources do not provide arbitrary input-dependent unary signatures. Planar
models retain their own promises and port or embedding requirements.

Approximation, sampling, parameterized counting, and fine-grained complexity have
independent guarantees. The exact dichotomies do not establish those guarantees.
Tractable structural branches and hardness branches also do not prove an
unconditional separation between FP and #P-hard problems.

The [API guide](api.md), [algorithm contracts](algorithms.md), and
[classification scope](flagships.md) locate the corresponding interfaces.
